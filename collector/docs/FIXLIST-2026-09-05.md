## Your question: how best to get the latest data, with prices

### Where the latency actually is

Measured on production, and measured properly — from `ingested_at` on each row,
not from how old the newest row looks.

| stage | typical | worst | bounded by |
|---|---|---|---|
| ride ends → trip row exists | **52 min** (median of 33 sampled) | **4 h 27 m** | not the provider. Undisturbed the cluster is 14–45 min; the tail is the collector's own queue |
| trip row → Uber price on it | **10.9 h** | **24.1 h by design** | the fare walk runs only on the nightly catch-up and the Sunday backfill |
| trip row → price (hotel) | 6–19 min | — | priced on the row as it lands |
| trip row → price (Bolt) | 11–16 min | — | fine in steady state |
| priced row → rollup → page | ≤ 15 min | — | the rollup cron |

### The three things actually holding it back

**1. One serial queue.** `runWindow` (`src/run.js:64-79`) is a *single* global
promise chain shared by the half-hourly incremental, the nightly catch-up, the
weekly backfill and every on-demand job — and `runWindowInner` then walks all
eight sources serially inside it. Backfill job 50 held that chain for **15.4
hours**. That is the entire difference between the 14–45 minute cluster and the
4 h 27 m tail. It also starves `reconcile()`, which runs as the *last statement*
of a collection pass, so the unauthorised detector stops re-judging anything for
as long as a backfill runs.

**2. The catch-up and the backfill are not durable.**
`cron.schedule('0 21 * * *', () => catchUp(30))` and the Sunday backfill pass no
`jobId`, so neither gets a checkpoint and neither is requeued at boot — only
admin-triggered jobs are. The container restarts on every deploy. Measured: every
source's catch-up run row was **30.7 hours old**; the 4 September run produced no
row at all and was never retried. That is the pass that pulls Uber fares.

**3. The fare walk is bolted to the wrong pass.** `src/sources/uber.js:1776`
skips the whole fare phase when `mode === 'incremental'`. The reasoning was the
payments report's generation cap — but that cap is **concurrency, not quota**
("wait for current reports to complete"), and `generateReport()` already backs
off on it with 60/120/180/240 s retries. Measured live: the in-flight cap is
**3 per org**, not the documented 6.

### What to do, in order

Three independent judges scored four designs and converged on this.

| # | step | effort | buys |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Make the catch-up and backfill `collector_job` rows so the existing claim/checkpoint/requeue path runs them | small | turns a ~24 h design ceiling into a real one instead of a floor |
| 2 | Two-level queue: an incremental or catch-up takes the next slot ahead of a running backfill; collapse duplicate queued incrementals | small | trip rows back to ~31 min worst **at all times** |
| 3 | Slice the Sunday backfill into a nightly two-month rotation | medium | caps one run's queue occupancy at ~1 h instead of 15.4 h |
| 4 | Take `events` (GDELT, which times out on every pass anyway) off the half-hourly incremental | small | ~135 s off a ~230 s pass |
| 5 | **Fix the HTTP verb on the Realtime-Transactions probe** | small | nothing directly — it is the gate on step 6 |
| 6 | *If it answers:* a Realtime-Transactions collector on its own tick, outside the queue, written to its own column marked provisional | medium | **Uber per-trip money in 5–20 minutes** |
| 7 | *If it does not:* give the running fare week its own 4-hourly tick, gated on queue depth | medium | **Uber fare ~24 h → ~2 h** |
| 8 | Stamp `trip.priced_at` and `price_source` on the NULL→non-NULL transition | small | makes every claim above checkable instead of asserted |

**Step 5 is the whole hinge.** Uber publishes per-trip money at sub-minute
freshness on `POST /v1/vehicle-suppliers/transactions`, on the scope
`supplier.partner.payments` that `src/config.js:125` **already requests** and that
`/api/auth` shows healthy for both orgs. All three of our probes issue it as a
**GET**, get a 404, and record the conclusion "the provider has nothing". One
verb.

Its ceiling, stated honestly: ~5-minute floor, ranges of ≤15 minutes, a 24-hour
lookback, and it can never backfill. So the weekly payments walk stays for
history — this is a second, faster tier, not a replacement.

### Judges rejected these, and I agree

- Per-fleet parallelism in the history lane — doubles concurrent report pressure against a cap of 3.
- A `*/10` fast lane for Bolt/hotel/Yango — triples Bolt portal volume, and `reconcile` is the tail of `runWindowInner`.
- Moving the fare phase above the trips phase — only helps during a backfill, and interacts badly with the checkpoint.
- A wholesale migration of the report pipeline to OAuth REST — take the read-only half (ListReports / GetReportByReportID) and leave the rest.

### Ceilings nothing reaches

- The report path is never seconds-fresh: generate → poll → download a CSV.
- Three reports in flight per Uber org, shared across report types. Measured, not assumed.
- Uber report retention floor is **2025-03-17** for PAYMENTS_ORDER.
- Bolt company 142868 (Ecosine) and the Yango park entitlement sit in someone else's portal.

## Unauthorised trips: the complete picture, as they complete

You asked specifically about this. It is the worst-served feature in the product,
and not for the reason it looks like.

### It has been switched off since 2 September, and the page says "0"

Accusations ran 3, 4, 5 a day through August and then went to **0 on 2, 3, 4 and
5 September**. Every segment since carries
`unavailable_sources: yango` and the reason *"no bookings collected from yango in
this window, so a booking there cannot be ruled out"*.

`blockingChannels()` in `src/reconcile.js` lets **any channel that ever produced
one booking** veto every accusation in any window it is absent from. Yango has
**36 bookings in the entire database**, none since 1 September, and its
credential 403s on every pass — so the veto is permanent. The code cannot tell
*"we did not collect it"* from *"we collected it and there was nothing there"*.

`/api/unauthorized/summary` returns `unauthorized: 0` beside
`coverage.complete: true`. The page's headline goes further and says **"Every
journey in this window has a booking behind it"** — on windows where two thirds
were never judged at all.

### 59.6% of the segment table is a phantom

`reconcile()` reads telemetry `WHERE captured_at BETWEEN from AND to`, so a
journey straddling the window start is **truncated** — and the upsert key is
`(plate, started_at)`, so each new window edge mints another differently-cut copy
that nothing ever deletes.

**1,910 of 3,213 rows** over 2026-08-21 → 09-05 are strict sub-intervals of
another segment on the same plate, in 662 families of 2 to 23. One verified
example: plate L76101 on 21 August has **one unbroken occupied run** from
08:47:35 to 10:38:28 — stored as **15 rows for 3 real journeys**, seven of them
nested suffixes all ending at 10:38:28, each reasoned *"the journey runs past the
edge of available telemetry"*.

The rate is 58–74% on every day older than three days and 0–5% inside the
incremental window. So `#unauthorized` overstates every count, km and minute by
about **2.5x**: it reads "3,284 occupancy intervals" where 1,326 journeys
happened, "Matched to a booking 1,112" against 487 real.

### And it can only ever judge about a third of journeys

After de-duplicating, **30–48% of distinct journeys are judged**; **32–48% are
refused** as `partial`. And `partial` is triggered by idle time **outside** the
journey rather than a gap inside it — 530 of 532 de-duplicated partials fire on
that. The seat sensor exists on ~20 of 264 tracked vehicles, because
`src/reconcile.js:241` selects `WHERE source='cabman'` only.

**None of this is stated anywhere on the page.**

### What is NOT wrong — so the report stays fair

- The accusations that exist are ~98% sound. **0 of 59** are contradicted by an
  overlapping matched segment.
- Late arrival is **not** the dominant problem. Measured lag from `ingested_at`:
  Uber ≤ +32 min, hotel ≤ +46 min, Bolt 11–16 min. Well inside the 120-minute
  guard.
- **1 of 59 (1.7%)** is now explained by a booking that exists — and it is not a
  latency bug. L63970 on 1 September still reads *"nearest is a hotel trip 411
  min away"* while hotel booking `6a959a661698f7d77013d705`, **ingested four days
  before the verdict was written**, covers it end to end. It was clipped out
  because bookings are selected by `requested_at BETWEEN from−4h AND to+4h` and
  that job ran 14.5 hours. **6% of hotel bookings span more than 4 h** (p99 16.3
  h). The drill-down clips identically, so the "argue with the verdict" panel
  shows an empty list to anyone checking the accusation.

### The fix, in order

1. **Un-veto it.** Weight `blockingChannels()` by the channel's normal daily volume, or scope it to the segment's own Dubai day. Yango at 36 bookings ever must not silence a fleet doing 800 a day. *(small)*
2. **Stop minting phantoms.** Read telemetry with a 12-hour pad either side, build segments over that, persist only those starting inside the window. *(small)*
3. **Make the pass authoritative.** Delete the window's superseded rows in the same transaction as the upsert. *(medium)*
4. **Retract the 1,910 existing phantoms** with a `schema_once`-guarded migration, exactly as `sql/schema_v8.sql:59` already does for a previous retraction. *(small)*
5. **Match bookings by interval overlap**, not `requested_at` plus a fixed pad — in the reconciler *and* in `api/segment_routes.js`. *(small)*
6. **Say it on the page**: how many journeys were judged, how many refused and why, that a verdict is provisional, and when it was decided. *(medium)*

### The capability nobody is using

`FMS GetTripPassenger` is a **passenger-occupancy journey feed covering both
fleets with ~2 years of history**, already reachable with the credentials we
hold. The detector currently sees ~20 CABMAN-equipped cars. And CABMAN's
`SeatSensorValue` is a **passenger count 0–5**, flattened to a boolean at the
door — the count is >1 on 18.8% of occupied fixes.

## Most of the 258 reduce to seven mistakes

Fixing a root cause settles many findings at once. This is the order I would
work in.

### R1 — A denominator that does not follow its filter *(≈14 findings)*
The numerator honours the fleet or platform chip; the denominator does not.
`/api/supply/balance` returns **`online_h: 25,907` under every chip**, so Egari
reads 94% idle and Bolt 99%. `#unit`'s asset ledger returns all 273 plates with
the Egari chip set, so a fleet that owns 40 cars is told it has 239 that never
moved. `/api/corporate/summary` divides all-booking revenue by only-measured
distance and publishes AED 6.71/km where the matched figure is AED 3.97.
**Fix once:** bind the page's own predicate into both halves of every ratio, and
add one test that asserts the parts sum to the whole under each chip.

### R2 — Absent rendered as zero, usually in green *(≈20 findings)*
The product's own house rule — *absent with a reason, never zero* — is broken on
the pages that most need it. 32 of 93 working vehicles show **"Harsh events 0 /
0 per 100 km" in green** because the alert feed never watched them. `#compliance`
shows **"Driver licences expired 0"** in green when there are no licence dates at
all. `#low-performers` ranks **68 of 82 drivers at "AED 0"** while the same table
says the money is unmeasurable. Drivers with no rating get a red **"Rating
0.00"**.
**Fix once:** `alertRate()` and its siblings already take a `tracked` argument
and already know how to return absent — pass it at the four call sites that
don't.

### R3 — One serial queue *(≈8 findings)*
See the latency section. A backfill starves the incremental, the catch-up, and
`reconcile()` alike.

### R4 — A partial day compared against whole ones *(≈11 findings)*
`#day` reads a seven-hour-old today as a 90% collapse and blames the collectors.
`#compare` reads a 1.6-hour collection lag as a 35% fall — on the page built to
prevent exactly that; through the hours today was actually collected it was **up
50%**. `#slot` claims the fleet "correctly decided not to staff" an hour still in
the future. **`#day/2026-09-06` — tomorrow — prints "MONEY IN AED 12,718" and a
red −100% beside "This date is before anything was collected".**
**Fix once:** one shared "how much of this period has happened" helper, used by
every comparison.

### R5 — A window boundary that mints duplicates *(≈6 findings)*
The occupancy segments, and the same shape in `metric_break`, where 9 of 34
stored rows carry pre-backfill numbers and 5 of the 12 cards `#causes` renders
are stale.

### R6 — One human counted twice *(≈9 findings)*
**62 of the 171 drivers the product reports are one person filed twice on the
same car**; the folded headcount is 110. 70% of the "handovers" and 77% of the
idle hours are one person changing apps, not a car changing hands. The safety
leaderboard ranks the same human twice among the fleet's worst drivers. And the
roster has **three sizes on three pages — 434, 345, 289 — all captioned "on the
books"**.

### R7 — A caption that does not describe the figure above it *(≈30 findings)*
`#corridors`: *"Showing the 119 busiest of 108 corridors."* `#optimise` prints
the literal word **"Infinity"**. `#playbook` calls one figure both "a month" and
"over 5 days". `#compare` says Uber publishes no fare, twice, directly above a
table of AED 2,899 of Uber fares. Uber's day statement is printed on **Yango,
Bolt and Hotel** trip pages as "what the channel says" — a 5.2x overstatement on
the Yango page.

## What the audit got wrong

Ten findings were refuted on re-measurement. Recording them because an audit that
never returns "no" is not measuring anything.

1. **"CABMAN's `Status` is the seat sensor restated"** — the premise was backwards; production supports the opposite.
2. **"AED 289.79/hour vs 436 hours — a 36x contradiction"** — documented twice in `api/economics_routes.js:749-764`; the finder read neither comment.
3. **"Yango months print 0 bookings beside a four-figure payout"** — asserted about the Revenue page, which filters to `bookings > 0`. It does not do it.
4. **"Egari has 67,701 chargeable Uber bookings with zero fares"** — *no longer reproduces.* Egari is now at **41,040 priced, 59.5% coverage** over that exact range. The interleave fix from yesterday is working through the backlog.
5. **"`#reconcile`'s September is 6/5 of every other page"** — different windows by construction; `#reconcile` is whole calendar months and says so.
6. **"`/api/day`'s statement figure has been blank 14 days"** — deliberate and correctly labelled: the tile says "the operator's own ledger" and reads the operator's own ledger.
7. **"`#coverage` looks finished for ~6 s"** — measures 556 ms, and a skeleton is shown throughout.
8–10. Three splits where one half was a documented decision and the other half survived on its own.

Three more were **downgraded** rather than refuted, and the reasons are worth
keeping: the 148-vs-147 driver count is a one-person merge difference where both
folds cover all 3,164 trips; the AED 122,840-vs-112,470 gap is already explained
in forty lines on `#unit`'s landing tab (the defect is only that the assets tile
reuses the label "Money in"); and the roster's 434/345/289 are three deliberate
populations that merely share one caption.

   re-failing the same eight windows. Roughly 13,800 Ecosine bookings, some AED
   700k of fares, are recoverable. The "Known holes" row saying *"permanently —
   nothing, past retention"* must go.
